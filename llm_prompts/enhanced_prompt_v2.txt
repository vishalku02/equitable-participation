## Instructions

1. Read the whole transcript before coding, and read each line in its surrounding exchange.
2. Code every row. Adults (Ms. Gallagher, Ms. Benoit) are not coded: give them Whose = N/A. The speaker "Other" is a student who is not one of the two focal students.
3. First, divide the student lines into turns: a turn is one student's lines that develop one mathematical thought, even when other speakers interrupt. Give each turn an ID (T1, T2, ...). Then record Whose on every line of each turn, and record Level only on the last line of the turn, once the thought is complete. Leave Level blank on the turn's other lines. Adult rows get no turn ID.
4. Whose = Self, Other, or Unclear / not coded (use N/A for adults and for rows outside the construct). Builds on = the peer and row being built on, only when Whose = Other. Level = the codebook level for Self or Other, on the turn's last line only.
5. Use the working translations for Spanish rows, but treat them as unreviewed. Treat words such as "aquí", "este", "this", or "there" as references whose objects are not visible: do not claim what a student physically showed.
