We take a situated, sociocultural view of what counts as mathematical participation (Moschkovich, 2002). Bilingual learners draw on many resources as they participate, including Spanish and English, everyday and informal language, gestures, and objects such as cubes and drawings. A contribution can be mathematically meaningful even when it does not sound like formal mathematical English, is phrased in Spanish or a mix of languages, or is a short fragment that only makes sense alongside the surrounding talk. Interpret each contribution in the context of the task and the discussion, while recognizing what the transcript cannot establish: gestures and objects are not recorded, and some lines are unintelligible.
